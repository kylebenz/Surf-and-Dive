## The flight is part of the holiday

When the destination is twelve or more hours away, pretending travel time does not matter is a mistake. Your itinerary should absorb the journey rather than fight it.

## Stay longer

A short long-haul trip often feels like a race. If possible, give yourself enough nights to recover, enjoy the destination and accept a few imperfect ocean days. For destinations such as [French Polynesia](/destinations/french-polynesia/) or [Fiji](/destinations/fiji/), ten nights or more is a sensible minimum.

## Reduce transfers

One beautiful island with excellent water can be better than three islands connected by exhausting transfers. Luxury is often the removal of friction. [The Maldives](/destinations/maldives/) is a good example: once you arrive, the ocean is outside the door.

## Plan around the ocean window

Research the destination's surf and diving seasons before booking flights. Official destination sources can help establish the broad pattern, but exact conditions need current local verification. The [Best by Month](/best-by-month/) planner is a good starting point.

## Build a recovery day

The first day should not always be a major surf or dive day. Sleep, hydrate, explore the hotel and let your body catch up. Then start the ocean portion of the trip properly.

## And at the end

Divers should respect the no-fly interval recommended by their training agency before the flight home. Plan the last ocean day accordingly — a lagoon swim or a final surf rather than a deep dive.
