## It sounds perfect until you plan it badly

Surfing and diving are both ocean sports, but they do not automatically share the same ideal conditions. The best trip starts by identifying which activity is primary.

## Choose the anchor activity

If you are an advanced surfer travelling for a specific swell, plan the trip around surfing and use diving as the secondary activity. If marine life is the main attraction, reverse the order.

## Look for destinations with multiple coastlines

[Bali](/destinations/bali-nusa-penida/) is a useful example because surf and diving are spread across different parts of the island and nearby islands. [The Maldives](/destinations/maldives/) can also work well when reef activities are built into the resort experience. [Fiji](/destinations/fiji/) and [Madeira](/destinations/madeira/) are also strong combinations.

## Do not overpack the schedule

You do not need two ocean sessions every day. Alternate hard surf days, dive days and recovery days. You will enjoy both activities more.

### A simple seven-day pattern

- **Day 1**: Arrive, rest, easy snorkel.
- **Days 2-3**: Surf mornings, quiet afternoons.
- **Day 4**: Dive day.
- **Day 5**: Recovery: spa, walk, long lunch.
- **Day 6**: Follow the conditions: surf if it is working, dive if it is not.
- **Day 7**: Gentle final surf; no diving within the pre-flight interval.

## Build the luxury around recovery

Good sleep, food, hydration, massage, easy transport and comfortable accommodation are not extras. They are part of the performance plan.
