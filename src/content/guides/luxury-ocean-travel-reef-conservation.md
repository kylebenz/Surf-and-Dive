## Luxury should not mean taking more from the ocean

The ocean is the reason these destinations are valuable. That creates a simple responsibility: enjoy it without treating it as an unlimited resource.

Responsible travel does not mean your holiday has to become uncomfortable. It means making better choices.

## Start with behaviour in the water

Do not touch coral. Do not chase wildlife. Keep appropriate distance from animals. Follow marine-protected-area rules. Use local guides who understand the environment. These small decisions add up.

- Perfect your buoyancy before diving near fragile reef.
- Never stand on coral, even in shallow lagoons.
- Do not feed fish, sharks, rays or turtles.
- Avoid operators that crowd, bait or touch wildlife.
- Choose reef-safe sun protection and cover up with a rash guard.
- Reduce single-use plastic. Bring a refillable bottle.

## Choose operators carefully

Ask how a dive or surf operator handles waste, wildlife encounters, reef access and local employment. A premium operator should be able to explain its practices clearly.

## Respect protected areas

Places such as [Fernando de Noronha](/destinations/fernando-de-noronha/), Hanifaru Bay in [the Maldives](/destinations/maldives/) and Tubbataha in [the Philippines](/destinations/philippines/) limit access precisely so that they stay extraordinary. Fees, time slots and visitor caps are part of the experience, not an inconvenience.

## Spend locally

Luxury travel can support communities when travellers choose locally owned restaurants, guides, craftspeople and experiences alongside international resorts.

## The real souvenir

The best ocean holiday is one where the place still feels alive after you leave. If you can return years later and find the same reef, beach and marine life, that is a much better measure of luxury than another room upgrade. Read our full [responsible travel principles](/responsible-travel/).
